Practice Questions

Q1. Which phrase means “in the meantime”?

  1. en route
  2. interim
  3. pro bono
  4. ad hoc

Q2. The Latin term “caveat emptor” translates to:

  1. let the buyer beware
  2. let the seller beware
  3. buyer’s remorse
  4. seller’s guarantee

Q3. Choose the expression that denotes “a temporary solution”:

  1. fait accompli
  2. ad interim
  3. de facto
  4. status quo

Q4. The French phrase “joie de vivre” literally means:

  1. joy of living
  2. joy of eating
  3. joy of traveling
  4. joy of learning

Q5. Which term refers to “a person who pretends to have knowledge”?

  1. dilettante
  2. virtuoso
  3. connoisseur
  4. savant

Q6. The German word “Schadenfreude” describes:

  1. pleasure from others’ misfortune
  2. fear of failure
  3. love of order
  4. respect for tradition

Q7. Select the phrase that means “by virtue of one’s position”:

  1. ex officio
  2. pro tem
  3. in situ
  4. ad infinitum

Q8. The Italian term “dolce far niente” translates to:

  1. sweet doing nothing
  2. sweet far away
  3. sweet far nothing
  4. sweet far something

Q9. Which expression is used to indicate “for the time being”?

  1. pro tempore
  2. in flagrante
  3. sub rosa
  4. per se

Q10. The phrase “carpe diem” originates from which language?

  1. Greek
  2. Latin
  3. Spanish
  4. Portuguese

Answer Key with Explanations

Q1. Answer: (b) interim – “Interim” directly means the intervening period.

Q2. Answer: (a) let the buyer beware – Classical Latin legal maxim warning purchasers.

Q3. Answer: (b) ad interim – “Ad interim” signifies a provisional arrangement.

Q4. Answer: (a) joy of living – French “joie” (joy) + “de vivre” (of living).

Q5. Answer: (a) dilettante – Refers to an amateur who dabbles without deep expertise.

Q6. Answer: (a) pleasure from others’ misfortune – German “Schaden” (harm) + “Freude” (joy).

Q7. Answer: (a) ex officio – Latin for “from the office,” meaning by virtue of position.

Q8. Answer: (a) sweet doing nothing – Italian “dolce” (sweet) + “far niente” (doing nothing).

Q9. Answer: (a) pro tempore – Latin “for the time being,” often abbreviated “pro tem.”

Q10. Answer: (b) Latin – From Horace’s Odes, “carpe diem” means “seize the day.”

Common Mistakes

  • Confusing “ad hoc” (for a particular purpose) with “ad interim” (temporary).
  • Assuming “dilettante” implies expertise rather than superficial interest.
  • Misreading “ex officio” as “former officer” instead of “by virtue of office.”
