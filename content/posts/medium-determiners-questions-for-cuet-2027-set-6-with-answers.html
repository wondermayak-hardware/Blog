Answer Key with Explanations

Q1. Answer: (a) All - "All" refers to the whole group of students collectively.

Q2. Answer: (a) no - Negative sentences use "no" before a singular noun to indicate zero quantity.

Q3. Answer: (b) Either - "Either" is used for one of two alternatives.

Q4. Answer: (a) many - "Many" modifies countable nouns in negative statements.

Q5. Answer: (a) Every - "Every" distributes the requirement to each individual student.

Q6. Answer: (a) any - In negative clauses, "any" is the correct determiner for singular countable nouns.

Q7. Answer: (a) Much - "Much" quantifies uncountable nouns like "information."

Q9. Answer: (a) little - "Little" expresses a small amount of uncountable "time."

Q10. Answer: (a) Each - "Each" emphasizes individual candidates within a group.

Common Mistakes

  • Using "much" with countable nouns instead of "many."
  • Confusing "either" and "neither" in positive versus negative contexts.
  • Placing "every" before plural nouns.

Quick Tips

  • Identify whether the noun is countable or uncountable before choosing a quantifier.
  • Remember that "any" appears in questions and negatives; "some" appears in affirmatives.
  • Use demonstratives (this/that/these/those) to match proximity and number.
  • Practice converting sentences between singular and plural to test determiner agreement.

FAQs

What is the difference between "each" and "every"?

"Each" highlights individual members of a group, while "every" treats the group as a whole; both take singular verbs.

When should I use "few" versus "a few"?

"Few" implies almost none (negative sense), whereas "a few" means a small but positive number.

Can "any" be used in affirmative sentences?

Generally no; "any" is standard in questions, negatives, and conditional clauses, while "some" fits affirmatives.
