Quick Answer: Use seven days: foundations, satellite series, flagship missions, human spaceflight and future projects, launch vehicles, then a practice test on day 6 and revision of weak areas on day 7. Make short notes with mission, year and purpose.

Preparing for the Space and ISRO portion of CUET 2027 can feel overwhelming, but a focused seven‑day schedule makes the task manageable and builds confidence.

Understand the Syllabus First

Start by downloading the official CUET General Test syllabus (General Knowledge and Current Affairs) and highlight every topic that falls under space science and ISRO missions. Typical items include major satellite launches, launch vehicle families, key missions such as Chandrayaan, Mangalyaan, Gaganyaan, and recent developments like Aditya‑L1. Knowing the exact scope prevents wasted time on peripheral material and lets you allocate study hours precisely.

Build a 7‑Day Study Plan

A day‑wise plan keeps momentum high and ensures coverage of all high‑weight areas. Follow the schedule below, adjusting only if a particular day feels too light or too heavy.

  1. Day 1 – Foundations: Read a concise overview of India’s space programme history, organisational structure of ISRO, and the role of the Department of Space. Make one‑page notes on each launch vehicle (SLV, ASLV, PSLV, GSLV, GSLV‑MkIII).
  2. Day 2 – Satellite Series: Focus on communication, remote sensing, navigation, and scientific satellites. List at least three satellites per category with launch year and primary purpose.
  3. Day 3 – Flagship Missions: Deep‑dive into Chandrayaan‑1, Chandrayaan‑2, Chandrayaan‑3, Mangalyaan, Astrosat, and Aditya‑L1. Note objectives, outcomes, and any world‑first achievements.
  4. Day 4 – Human Spaceflight & Future Projects: Study Gaganyaan mission design, astronaut selection, life‑support systems, and upcoming missions like Shukrayaan and the Indian Space Station concept.
  5. Day 5 – Launch Vehicle Evolution: Compare payload capacities, stages, and propellant types across PSLV, GSLV, and GSLV‑MkIII. Create a quick‑reference table for revision.
  6. Day 6 – Practice Questions & Mock Test: Solve at least 30 multiple‑choice questions from previous CUET papers and reputable question banks. Time yourself to simulate exam conditions.
  7. Day 7 – Rapid Revision & Weak‑Area Fix: Review all notes, flashcards, and the mock‑test analysis. Spend extra time on any topic where accuracy fell below 70 %.

Use Reliable Resources

Stick to a limited set of trustworthy sources to avoid information overload. The ISRO official website and its annual reports provide accurate, exam‑oriented content. Complement these with a single well‑reviewed GK compendium that contains a dedicated space section. Avoid random blogs or unverified videos; they often contain outdated or incorrect data.

Practice with Previous Papers

Past CUET papers reveal the question pattern, difficulty level, and frequently tested facts. Allocate one hour on Day 6 to solve a full‑length GK section under timed conditions. After the test, mark every wrong answer, categorize the error (knowledge gap, misreading, careless), and update your notes accordingly. This targeted review is far more effective than re‑reading entire chapters.

Revise Smartly

Active recall beats passive reading. Convert each note into a question‑answer flashcard (digital or paper). Use spaced repetition: review Day 1 cards on Day 3, Day 2 cards on Day 4, and so on. On the final day, run through the entire deck in random order. Keep a “mistake log” – a one‑page list of facts you repeatedly miss – and glance at it just before the exam.

Stay Healthy and Motivated

Seven days of intense study can drain mental energy. Sleep at least seven hours, eat balanced meals, and take a five‑minute stretch break every hour. A short walk or breathing exercise clears the mind and improves retention. Remind yourself that consistent effort, not perfection, drives improvement. Celebrate small wins – completing a day’s plan, scoring higher on the mock – to keep morale high.

Practice Questions

Question 1

Which launch vehicle carried the Chandrayaan‑3 mission to the Moon?
Answer: GSLV‑MkIII (also called LVM‑3).

Question 2

What is the primary objective of the Aditya‑L1 mission?
Answer: To study the solar corona, solar wind, and the Sun’s magnetic field from the Lagrange point 1.

Question 3

Name the Indian navigation satellite system and its first satellite.
Answer: The system is NavIC (Navigation with Indian Constellation) and its first satellite, IRNSS‑1A, was launched in 2013.

FAQs

How many hours should I study each day?

Aim for 4–5 focused hours split into two sessions with a short break. Quality matters more than sheer quantity.

Can I skip the human spaceflight topic if I run out of time?

Human spaceflight carries moderate weight; if time is extremely limited, prioritize flagship missions and launch vehicles first, then return to Gaganyaan basics.

Are current affairs on space relevant for CUET 2027?

Yes. Recent launches, mission milestones and policy updates often appear as one‑mark questions. Allocate a brief daily scan of ISRO press releases.

What if my mock‑test score is low on Day 6?

Analyse the errors, focus Day 7 on those weak spots, and remember that a single mock does not define final performance. Use it as a diagnostic tool, not a verdict.
