Q1. Under Article 131, the Supreme Court has original jurisdiction over which type of disputes?

Answer Key with Explanations

Q1. Answer: (a) disputes between states - Article 131 grants the Supreme Court original jurisdiction over inter‑state disputes.

Q2. Answer: (b) any legal right - Article 226 allows High Courts to issue writs for enforcement of any legal right, not only fundamental rights.

Q3. Answer: (b) Chief Justice of India and the four senior‑most judges - The collegium comprises the CJI and the four senior‑most Supreme Court judges.

Q4. Answer: (b) Any citizen can file a PIL on behalf of a group - Indian courts have relaxed locus standi, permitting any public‑spirited person to file a PIL.

Q5. Answer: (b) Strike down constitutional amendments that violate the basic structure - The basic structure doctrine empowers the Supreme Court to invalidate amendments altering the Constitution’s core features.

Q6. Answer: (a) 7 - The original Constitution fixed the Supreme Court strength at a Chief Justice plus seven other judges.

Q7. Answer: (c) Both review and curative petitions - The Supreme Court entertains a review petition first; a curative petition is available thereafter on limited grounds.

Q8. Answer: (b) Appeal lies to the Supreme Court - Decisions of the NGT can be appealed directly to the Supreme Court under the NGT Act.

Q9. Answer: (b) Supreme Court to examine constitutionality of legislative acts - Judicial review is the power of the Supreme Court (and High Courts) to test laws against the Constitution.

Q10. Answer: (b) Two‑thirds majority - Removal of a Supreme Court judge requires a two‑thirds majority of members present and voting in each House.

Common Mistakes

  • Confusing original and appellate jurisdiction of the Supreme Court.
  • Assuming only aggrieved persons can file a PIL.
  • Mixing up the collegium composition with the executive appointment process.

FAQs

What is the best way to remember the number of original Supreme Court judges?

Recall that the Constitution originally fixed the strength at a Chief Justice plus seven other judges.

Can a High Court issue writs for non‑fundamental rights?

Yes, Article 226 empowers High Courts to issue writs for any legal right, not only fundamental rights.

How does a curative petition differ from a review petition?

A curative petition is a final remedy after a review petition is dismissed, allowed only on rare grounds like violation of natural justice.
